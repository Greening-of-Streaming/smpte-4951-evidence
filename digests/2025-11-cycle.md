## 1. Campaign metadata

**REM half — the November 2025 hackathon rerun** (reference [2],
"Hackathon Data Analysis", Greening of Streaming, February 2026; 13
contributors: Soltaninezhad, Koster, Lange, Schwarz, Robinson, Mehta,
Bullett, Palmero, Mattera, Jones, Moote, Pouli, Siglin).

- **Testbed:** TNO servers — AMD Opteron 6328, 16 CPUs, 130 GB; Debian 12;
  encoder ffmpeg 7.1.7 (x264 `-preset veryfast`, no GPU), packager
  MediaMTX 1.15.1 (fMP4 HLS); RTMP push encoder→packager; 10-min cron
  schedule.
- **Conditions:** resolutions 1920×1080 / 960×540 / 480×270; bitrates
  1 / 6 / 12 Mb/s; 30 fps; one codec (x264). 4K was *not* run.
- **Energy-signature sequence (6 min, 7 segments):** noise 30 s ×2
  (warm-up, max complexity) · black 30 · white 30 · black 30 (luminance
  landmarks) · El Fuente excerpt 180 s (content window) · black 30
  (end marker). Segment lengths chosen so every end device gets ≥ 3
  power samples per segment.
- **Capture architecture as it was in November 2025:**
  - *Server side:* PDU SNMP (Schleifenbauer MIB) at **4 s**, current in
    cA × voltage in cV → W (PDU native power resolution 10 W, hence the
    current/voltage route); CSV → Excel.
  - *End devices:* Tapo P110 per TV/player, read through the **TP-Link
    cloud API** by the REM collector — **10 s sampling, 1 W resolution**;
    the cloud path is the limiting factor ([2] §4 states the local path
    gives mW at up to 1 s but it was not used). Data processing: trim to
    the minimum common sample count (**150 samples per condition**,
    i.e. ~25 min of 10-s samples pooled), re-zero time, 7-sample moving
    average for plots, ANOVA + η² for effect sizes. Cloud-API rate
    limits set the polling floor (REM's own tests, 2025-12-02, 34
    devices: 5 s breaks, 20 s runs reliably, 30 s production default —
    `POLLING_FINAL_RESULTS.md`).
  - *Network:* not measured in this rerun (gateway measured once, in
    Hackathon 4c).
- **Players:** TVs' built-in browsers or external players (VLC on a Pi)
  on monitors; make/model/size/panel type recorded per participant;
  4 TV models in the testbed (per the 2026-07 REM audit).

**OWL half — the pricing turn.** Reference [3] (Pouli et al., #4941,
these proceedings) is the codec/energy analysis on OWL. The wattlab

NVENC tuned} × bitrate ladder × {BBB, Meridian}, 1080p, 30-s clips

## 3. Findings

### F1 — Resolution is the dominant server-side driver (REM found it)

- **Claim:** encoder power rises strongly with resolution; **resolution
  accounts for ~55 % of the observed variance in encoding power**
  (ANOVA/η²); packaging changes only slightly with resolution.
- **Source:** [2] Executive summary and "Initial Interpretation" —
  **not re-derivable on GoS1**: the November 2025 samples are not in the
  production REM database (earliest row 2026-02-08, post-migration to
  Linode; the earlier InfluxDB→TimescaleDB migration and the December
  2025 data loss predate it). Any re-analysis needs the CSVs held by the
  analysts (Tania).
- **Status:** 🟡 Early Insight as GoS's own claim (one testbed, one
  codec, numbers only via [2]); [2] itself calls the effect
  "statistically robust".

### F2 — Bitrate is a strong secondary, strongest at the packager (REM)

- **Claim:** bitrate raises both encoder and packager power; the
  packager's bitrate dependence is the more *consistent* effect (large
  effect size) even though its absolute range (~a dozen watts) is
  smaller than the encoder's (a few tens of watts). Practical reading:
  resolution mainly moves encode, bitrate moves both, especially
  packaging — report the two separately.
- **Source / status:** [2] only; 🟡.

### F3 — Device-side bitrate/resolution effects are small; luminance is the strongest correlate (REM)

- **Claim:** after per-device normalisation (1080p30 @ 6 Mb/s baseline),
  pooled mean shifts are 1–4 % (largest 3.71 %; largest mean z-score
  0.087); **no display shows a significant bitrate or resolution effect
  at p < 0.05**. Mean frame luminance explains r² ≈ 0.47 (z-scored
  power) / 0.35 (% vs baseline) of device-power variance; texture/edge
  proxies r² ≈ 0.06–0.13. Past hackathons: 4–10 W (≤ 10 %) extra on some
  4K TVs upscaling HD (not rerun).
- **Source / status:** [2] only; 🟡. This is the observation C4 sharpened
  into a null with CIs and C6/C11 explained.

### F4 — Hardware implementation moves encode energy more than codec choice, in some configurations (OWL priced it)

- **Claim (wattlab S53 artifact):** NVENC used **2.5–4.4× less Wh/min
  than the CPU encoder** for the same codec at the same bitrate ladder
  (largest for H.265: 0.16 vs 0.71 Wh/min at 1080p) — the saving is
  speed, not lower draw (ΔW ≈ 70 W on both paths). Across the full
  5-rung ladder at VMAF 92 on the low-complexity content: H.264 CPU
  1.03 / GPU 0.73 Wh/min, H.265 1.91 / 0.79, AV1 1.37 / 0.74. The
  CPU-vs-GPU gap (≈ 1.4–2.4× on the ladder, up to 4.4× single-rung)
  is as large as or larger than the codec-to-codec gap on the same
  hardware (CPU: H.265 1.9× H.264; GPU: ≈ 1.1×) — i.e. **where the
  encoder runs decides the bill at least as much as which codec**. The
  quality side: NVENC reaches CPU VMAF except on low-complexity content
  at low bitrate (AV1/BBB gap up to −8.9 VMAF), and the "tuned" NVENC
  bundle costs 1.6–2.8× energy for no VMAF gain on H.264/AV1.
- **Which track:** measured and interpreted by OWL; the *operational*
  significance (resolution/bitrate are the variables that matter in
  deployment) comes from REM F1–F3. The full codec analysis is [3].
- **Status:** 🟢 within panel (90 + 48 rows, every row 🟢 by OWL's
  per-run confidence; one server, one GPU generation, 1080p);
  🟡 beyond it. **#4941 boundary:** cite [3] for the codec figures; this
  paper may report the *framework* result (hardware path vs codec) with
  the artifact named — confirm with Tania (`4941_BOUNDARY_ASK.md`).
