## 4. The 8-minute sequence: arithmetic behind the design

The submitted outline states REM "converged on a practical 8-minute test
sequence … to accommodate up to twenty sub-sequences … accepting a
reduced polling sample count per segment". **No 8-minute sequence
exists as a file on GoS1**; the sequences actually fielded were **6 min**
(Nov 2025, 7 segments × 30 s + 180 s content) and **10 min** (July 2026,
hackathon: B/W/B 3 × 30 s + ~6.5-min content + 60-s tail; also what
OWL's `/prepare-rem` builds). The arithmetic for the design as stated:

- 8 min = 480 s; 20 sub-sequences → **24 s each**.
- Cloud path, 10-s tick, per-device offset up to ~2 s within a slot:
  **2 samples per sub-sequence** (sometimes 3), at 1-W resolution —
  below the "≥ 3 samples per segment" rule [2] used, and below C4's
  "markers ≥ 6× the poll interval" lesson (30-s markers gave 3 samples
  and still needed a ±20-s lag search). 20 sub-sequences at 10 s is
  only viable if each is ≥ 60 s (→ 20 min) or the cadence drops.
- LEM path at 1 s (or 2 s): **24 (12) samples per sub-sequence**, at
  mW — the 8-minute / twenty-sub-sequence design is feasible *only* on
  the LAN path; on the cloud path the same 8 minutes supports ~8
  sub-sequences of 60 s.
- Fleet default (30 s) is not usable for signature work at all
  (< 1 sample per 24-s sub-sequence).

That is the retro-justification Section 3 wants: the constraint (cloud
tick) fixed the *segment* length at 30 s and the count at 7 (Nov 2025);
removing it (LEM) is what makes short sub-sequences — and many of them —
countable. R2/C8 (`digests/2026-08-r2-dual-capture.md`) measures the
same playback on both paths to put numbers on "what survives at 10 s".
